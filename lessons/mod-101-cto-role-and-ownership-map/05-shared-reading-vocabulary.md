# Shared Reading Vocabulary — Majors, Horowitz, Fournier

## Motivation

The rest of this curriculum leans on a small set of essays and
book chapters as *shared vocabulary*. When mod-104 says "the
promote-vs-hire trade-off for your first EM", it assumes you
have read Fournier on the manager career track. When mod-108
says "the hard-thing decisions are the ones with no good
option, only least-bad ones", it assumes you have read
Horowitz. When mod-106 says "the CTO's calendar is a signal —
if you are swinging back to IC work, do it on purpose", it
assumes you have read Majors on the engineer/manager pendulum.

This chapter names the four load-bearing readings, explains
*why* each one is quoted in later modules, and gives a reading
order that leaves you ready to run mod-102 onwards without
having to context-switch mid-module. None of the four is
optional. All four are widely available (three books, one set
of essays), and — importantly — none of them requires you to
buy a course, sign an NDA, or accept a fractional-CTO sales
pitch to read.

## The four readings

### Charity Majors — *What is a CTO?* and *The Engineer/Manager Pendulum* (essays)

- **Primary link** — Majors's essay collection at
  [charity.wtf](https://charity.wtf/). The two specific
  essays this curriculum quotes are:
  - *"WTF is a CTO, anyway?"* — the honest answer to "what
    is a CTO" and the argument that the seat is defined by
    the company's needs rather than a canonical job
    description.
  - *"Engineering Management: The Pendulum Or The Ladder"*
    ([charity.wtf/2019/01/04](https://charity.wtf/2019/01/04/engineering-management-the-pendulum-or-the-ladder/))
    — the argument that senior engineers and engineering
    managers benefit from *swinging* between IC and management
    seats over a career, rather than treating the switch as a
    one-way promotion.
- **Why this curriculum builds on it** — chapter 01's rung
  descriptions and chapter 02's role-split framing both use
  Majors's "the job is what the company needs it to be"
  framing. The Series-B+ discussion of when a CTO deliberately
  swings back to a staff-engineer seat (mod-106, chapter 04 of
  this module) uses the pendulum vocabulary.
- **Read this before** — mod-102, so you have the vocabulary
  the later modules assume.

### Ben Horowitz — *The Hard Thing About Hard Things*

- **Primary link** — HarperCollins publisher page:
  [harpercollins.com](https://www.harpercollins.com/products/the-hard-thing-about-hard-things-ben-horowitz).
  Read the whole book if you can; the chapters that matter
  most to this curriculum are:
  - The distinction between *peacetime CEO* and *wartime CEO*
    (applies to the CTO seat too).
  - The chapter on management debt.
  - The chapters on the hard conversations — laying people
    off, demoting a friend, firing an executive.
  - The chapter on hiring executives ("hiring VPs").
- **Why this curriculum builds on it** — Horowitz's
  vocabulary of "hard-thing decisions" (the ones with no good
  option, only least-bad ones) is the vocabulary mod-105 uses
  for the deprecate-vs-rewrite-vs-leave decision on technical
  debt and the vocabulary mod-108 uses for the co-founder
  dispute mechanic and hostile technical due diligence.
- **Read this before** — mod-104 (for the hiring-executives
  chapter) and mod-108 (for the hard-conversations chapters).
  Even a first pass is enough; the book is one you re-read
  under crisis, not one you cram once.

### Camille Fournier — *The Manager's Path*, CTO chapter

- **Primary link** — O'Reilly publisher page:
  [oreilly.com — The Manager's Path](https://www.oreilly.com/library/view/the-managers-path/9781491973882/).
  The whole book is the canonical management-career-ladder
  text; the chapter this curriculum leans on hardest is the
  CTO chapter, which walks the CTO / VP Eng distinction and
  the different flavours of CTO seat (technical co-founder,
  hands-on CTO, executive CTO).
- **Why this curriculum builds on it** — chapter 02 of this
  module (CTO vs. VP Eng vs. Chief Architect vs. Head of
  Platform) uses Fournier's definitions almost verbatim.
  Mod-104's material on first-EM and first-VP-Eng decisions
  builds on Fournier's manager-career-ladder framing.
- **Read this before** — mod-104. Fournier's ladder
  vocabulary is the vocabulary mod-104's career-ladder-v0
  material inherits.

### Will Larson — *An Elegant Puzzle* and *Staff Engineer*

- **Primary link** —
  [lethain.com/elegant-puzzle](https://lethain.com/elegant-puzzle/)
  and [staffeng.com/book](https://staffeng.com/book).
  Both books are cited across the curriculum:
  - *An Elegant Puzzle* — for the systems-of-engineering-
    management vocabulary that shows up in mod-104 and
    mod-106.
  - *Staff Engineer* — for the *staff-archetype* vocabulary
    (Tech Lead, Architect, Solver, Right Hand) that mod-104's
    founding-engineer-profile material and chapter 02's
    Chief-Architect-vs-Staff-Engineer discussion both use.
- **Why this curriculum builds on it** — the staff-archetype
  taxonomy is the language the founding-engineer profile in
  mod-104 uses. The systems-of-management vocabulary is the
  language mod-106's stage-transition playbooks use.
- **Read this before** — mod-104. Larson's material is a
  natural companion volume to Fournier's — one is the
  manager-track ladder, the other is the IC-track ladder,
  and both are needed to run the mod-104 hiring-plan work.

## Reading order

If you have a week: read the two Majors essays (an hour), the
Fournier CTO chapter (an hour), and the Horowitz chapters on
wartime/peacetime CEO + hiring VPs + management debt (an
afternoon). That is enough vocabulary to start mod-102.

If you have a month: add the full *Hard Thing About Hard
Things* (a weekend), the full *Manager's Path* (a weekend),
and Larson's *An Elegant Puzzle* (a week — you will re-read
sections against mod-104 and mod-106 later).

If you have a quarter: add Larson's *Staff Engineer* and
begin re-reading whichever of the four you find yourself
quoting most often — that is the one you will need to
internalise most deeply.

## Other readings this curriculum cites but does not require up front

The following are cited in later modules and are worth
knowing exist, but are *not* required before mod-102:

- **Elad Gil — *High Growth Handbook*** —
  [growth.eladgil.com](https://growth.eladgil.com/).
  Free to read online; the executive-scaling chapters
  matter most at rung (d), so mod-106 and mod-108
  reference it, but you don't need it for mod-102.
- **Matthew Skelton and Manuel Pais — *Team Topologies*** —
  [teamtopologies.com/book](https://teamtopologies.com/book).
  Cited throughout mod-104; read alongside mod-104 rather
  than up front.
- **Forsgren, Humble, Kim — *Accelerate*** and the current
  DORA State of DevOps report ([dora.dev](https://dora.dev/)).
  Cited throughout mod-106; read alongside mod-106.
- **Michael Nygard — "Documenting Architecture Decisions"** —
  [cognitect.com — Documenting Architecture Decisions](https://cognitect.com/blog/2011/11/15/documenting-architecture-decisions).
  Cited in mod-102; short enough to read the first time
  you sit down to author an ADR.
- **Simon Brown — C4 Model** —
  [c4model.com](https://c4model.com/). Cited in mod-102.
- **Ford, Parsons, Kua — *Building Evolutionary
  Architectures*** — cited in mod-102 and mod-105.
- **Zach Goldberg — *The Startup CTO's Handbook*** —
  open source at
  [github.com/ZachGoldberg/Startup-CTO-Handbook](https://github.com/ZachGoldberg/Startup-CTO-Handbook).
  Complementary to this curriculum; useful as a reference
  for founder-scope operational hygiene the curriculum
  points at but does not re-teach.
- **Y Combinator — Startup School Library** —
  [startupschool.org/library](https://www.startupschool.org/library).
  Video / essay library the whole startup-track ecosystem
  builds on; consumed alongside `startup-foundations` as a
  shared prerequisite.

## Summary

- Four load-bearing readings: Majors's essays, Horowitz's
  *Hard Thing About Hard Things*, Fournier's *Manager's
  Path* (CTO chapter), Larson's *An Elegant Puzzle* +
  *Staff Engineer*.
- Reading them up front gives you the vocabulary mod-102
  through mod-108 assume — running the later modules
  without the shared vocabulary means the material lands
  ~40% weaker.
- If you have only a week before mod-102, prioritise the
  Majors essays, the Fournier CTO chapter, and the
  Horowitz chapters on wartime/peacetime + hiring VPs +
  management debt.
- The other cited works (Skelton & Pais, Forsgren et al.,
  Nygard, Brown, Ford et al., Goldberg, YC library) are
  read alongside the modules that reference them, not up
  front.

There is no exercise for this chapter — the exercise *is*
the reading. Note the four titles in your personal-plan
artifact from chapter 04 as prerequisite reading before
mod-102, and pick a cadence for consuming them.

`resources.md` in this module directory lists all of the
above with primary links.
