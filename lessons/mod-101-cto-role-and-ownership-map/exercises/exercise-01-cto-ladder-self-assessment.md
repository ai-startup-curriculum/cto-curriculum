# Exercise 01 — CTO Ladder Self-Assessment

**Module:** `mod-101-cto-role-and-ownership-map`
**Planned time:** ~1 hour
**Chapter this builds on:** [`01-cto-ladder-pre-seed-to-series-b.md`](../01-cto-ladder-pre-seed-to-series-b.md)

## Problem statement

Locate yourself and your startup on the four-rung CTO ladder
from chapter 01 — honestly, from evidence rather than title —
and identify the specific mismatch (if any) between what your
company needs at its current stage and what your calendar
actually shows you doing.

If you are not currently in a CTO seat, run the assessment
against either (a) a real founder you know well enough to
answer the calendar questions for, or (b) a hypothetical
pre-seed technical co-founder or Series-A CTO you construct
from a real startup profile you understand (fictional
companies from press coverage do not qualify — pick a real
one).

## Requirements

Produce a **one-page written self-assessment** (roughly
300-600 words plus a filled-in table) containing all of the
following.

### 1. Stage anchor — what your company needs

- The company's funding stage (`IDEA / PRE-SEED / SEED /
  SERIES-A / GROWTH / MATURE`) using the vocabulary from
  [`startup-foundations`](https://github.com/ai-startup-curriculum/startup-foundations).
- Engineering headcount today (including yourself) and
  headcount 12 months ago.
- The rung the company *needs* the CTO to be at based on
  headcount + funding stage — one of the four archetypes
  from chapter 01 (technical co-founder / hands-on CTO /
  player-coach CTO / leadership-only CTO). State the rung
  and give one sentence of justification.

### 2. Calendar anchor — what you are actually doing

Sample your **last 4 calendar weeks** and estimate the split
across the following buckets. Total must add to 100%. Use
whatever evidence is available — calendar entries, Slack
history, PR history, GitHub contribution graph. If the
evidence disagrees with your memory, trust the evidence.

- Individual-contributor time (writing / reviewing code,
  responding to incidents as an operator, hands-on debugging).
- People-management time (1:1s, hiring loops, performance
  conversations, offer negotiations, career-ladder work).
- Architecture / technical-strategy time (ADRs, C4 diagrams,
  cross-team architectural review, build-vs-buy calls).
- CEO / board / external time (CEO 1:1s, board pre-reads,
  investor conversations, customer-executive briefings,
  analyst calls).
- Product / cross-functional time (roadmap review with the
  CPO, delivery-cadence rituals, cross-functional planning).
- Recruiting-marketing / community time (podcasts, blog
  posts, community talks, external technical narrative).
- Other (name it).

State the rung your calendar *implies* — again, one of the
four archetypes — with one sentence of justification.

Fill in the table from chapter 01:

| Column A — what the company needs at its stage | Column B — what your calendar / calendar-optional artifacts (Slack, PR history) actually show |
|---|---|
| (rung + one sentence) | (rung + one sentence) |

### 4. The mismatch (if any) and the remediation

- If A and B match: state that, note the *next* stage
  transition you are pointing at, and name the modules from
  chapter 04 you should be background-reading now.
- If A and B differ by exactly one rung: state which side is
  ahead (usually the calendar lags the company), name the
  single largest calendar-shape change you would make this
  quarter to close the gap, and name the module of this
  curriculum that addresses it.
- If A and B differ by more than one rung: state which side
  is ahead. Name the mismatch as the most important thing to
  fix this quarter, before the rest of the curriculum lands.
  Sketch a two-sentence remediation plan (typically involves
  either a hire or a deliberate handoff of work you are
  currently doing).

## Starter guidance

- Print or open your calendar for the last 4 weeks *before*
  you start writing. Do not estimate the calendar split from
  memory — the whole point of the exercise is to catch the
  gap between the story you tell yourself and the story your
  calendar tells.
- Do not use LinkedIn title as evidence. "CTO and
  co-founder" tells you nothing about which rung the calendar
  actually implies.
- If your calendar contains a very lumpy week (e.g. one
  full week at a customer offsite), either drop it and use
  the other three, or extend the sample to 6 weeks and drop
  the two lumpiest.
- If you are not currently a CTO but are constructing the
  assessment against a real founder you know, do the
  calendar sampling by asking them for a specific week's
  calendar — not by guessing.
- Keep the write-up on one page. If you find yourself
  writing more than a page, the extra material almost
  always belongs in your `exercise-03` personal-plan
  artifact, not here.

## Acceptance criteria

Your self-assessment is complete when a reader (a
co-founder, an executive coach, a mentor) can:

- Read the stage anchor and immediately tell which rung
  the company needs the CTO to be at, without asking a
  follow-up question.
- Read the calendar-anchor bucket split (with percentages
  summing to 100) and immediately tell which rung the
  calendar implies, without asking a follow-up question.
- Read the two-column table and see the mismatch (or its
  absence) at a glance.
- Read the remediation and understand what the single
  largest calendar-shape change is and why it is that
  one, not one of the alternatives.

The write-up is **not** a plan for the whole year — it is
a stage anchor. Its job is to make the rest of this
module's exercises — especially
[`exercise-03-personal-stage-by-stage-development-plan.md`](exercise-03-personal-stage-by-stage-development-plan.md)
— possible to author against something concrete.
