# Exercise 03 — Personal Stage-by-Stage Development Plan

**Module:** `mod-101-cto-role-and-ownership-map`
**Planned time:** ~2 hours
**Chapters this builds on:** [`04-personal-stage-by-stage-self-development-plan.md`](../04-personal-stage-by-stage-self-development-plan.md), and depends on the output of [`exercise-01`](exercise-01-cto-ladder-self-assessment.md) and [`exercise-02`](exercise-02-cto-vs-vp-eng-vs-chief-architect-decision-drill.md).

## Problem statement

Author your personal stage-by-stage development plan against
your current rung — the artifact you would share with a
co-founder, an executive coach, or a mentor to explain what
you are learning, in what order, and why. Use the
four-quadrant template from chapter 04 as the plan's
load-bearing artifact.

The plan is not a wish list. Every item in the "Now" bucket
must be justified by a real problem you have this quarter,
and every item in the "Next transition" bucket must be
justified by the specific transition threshold you are
approaching.

## Requirements

Produce a **two-to-three-page written plan** containing all
of the following.

### 1. Anchor summary (~1 paragraph)

Restate — in two or three sentences — the rung you are on
and the next transition you are approaching. Reference the
output of [`exercise-01`](exercise-01-cto-ladder-self-assessment.md)
and, if the mismatch remediation identified there is still
open, name it here. This is the frame the plan is authored
against.

### 2. The four-quadrant template — filled in

Use the template from chapter 04:

|  | This repo (`cto-curriculum`) | Peer tracks and higher-level tracks |
|---|---|---|
| **Now (this quarter)** | Modules you are consuming depth-first for a live problem | Peer-track modules your co-founder / first hires are consuming, that you are *routing* questions to |
| **Next transition** | Modules you are background-reading before the next stage transition | Higher-level-track modules you are *naming* (not yet studying) |

Each cell must contain:

- The module identifiers (e.g. `mod-104`, `mod-106
  (15→50 slice)`, `cpo-curriculum` deep discovery chapters,
  `ai-infra-senior-architect` level 45).
- For every "Now this repo" entry: a one-sentence statement
  of the *live problem* you expect that module to help you
  with this quarter.
- For every "Now peer tracks" entry: the name of the
  co-founder, first hire, or advisor who is doing that
  reading (not you).
- For every "Next this repo" entry: the transition
  threshold it points at (crossing 5 engineers, crossing 15,
  crossing 50, first EM split, first Chief Architect
  split, Series-B fundraise, etc.).
- For every "Next higher-level tracks" entry: a single
  sentence naming what the ladder looks like past this repo
  (not a reading assignment — a landmark).

### 3. The peer-track routing table

A short table of the peer tracks named above with the
answer to "*if a question in this pillar arrives on my
calendar, who do I route it to?*":

| Pillar | Peer track | Who is reading it | How I route the question |
|---|---|---|---|
| Product | `cpo-curriculum` | ... | ... |
| Strategy / CEO | `founder-ceo-curriculum` | ... | ... |
| People / Ops | `startup-operations-governance-curriculum` | ... | ... |
| Finance | `startup-finance-fundraising-curriculum` | ... | ... |
| GTM / Sales | `startup-product-gtm-curriculum` | ... | ... |
| Governance | `startup-operations-governance-curriculum` | ... | ... |

If a row has no owner other than you at your current stage,
write "*me, at shallow depth*" and note the shallow-depth
reading you are doing (typically a chapter from the
`startup-foundations` prerequisite plus the shallow slice
this repo teaches). Do not leave a row blank.

### 4. The shared-reading commitment (chapter 05)

State which of the four load-bearing readings from chapter
05 (Majors's essays, Horowitz's *Hard Thing About Hard
Things*, Fournier's *Manager's Path* CTO chapter, Larson's
*An Elegant Puzzle* + *Staff Engineer*) you have already
read and which are still on the list. For anything still
on the list, name a date by which you will finish it.
"Someday" is not a date; a specific week or month is.

### 5. Re-planning cadence

State — in a single sentence — the cadence at which you
will re-run this plan (chapter 04 recommends quarterly plus
on every stage transition). Add a calendar reminder or a
recurring task pointing at this file.

## Starter guidance

- Do not over-fill the "Now" quadrant. If everything is
  Now, nothing is Now. At most three modules should sit in
  the "Now this repo" cell — the ones you can genuinely
  consume depth-first in the coming quarter.
- The "Next transition" quadrant is *background reading*,
  not depth study. Skimming a chapter per week is enough at
  this altitude. If you find yourself deep-reading a
  "Next" module, either promote it to Now (because the
  transition is closer than you thought) or accept that
  the depth reading is optional and move on.
- If your co-founders are not reading the peer tracks named
  in the routing table, that is a finding — surface it in
  the plan and name the conversation you will have to close
  the gap. Do *not* silently absorb the peer-track scope
  yourself.
- Higher-level tracks in the "Next" quadrant should be
  *named*, not *studied*. Naming them is the artifact; the
  studying happens two or three stages from now.
- Do not skip the shared-reading commitment. If you have
  not yet read the four load-bearing texts, mod-102 through
  mod-108 will land ~40% weaker.

## Acceptance criteria

Your plan is complete when a reader (a co-founder, an
executive coach, a mentor) can:

- Read the anchor summary and understand what rung you are
  on and what transition you are pointing at.
- Read the four-quadrant table and see, at a glance, what
  you are consuming *now* vs. what you are background-reading
  vs. what your co-founders / first hires are consuming vs.
  what the ladder past this repo looks like.
- Read the peer-track routing table and see, for each
  non-CTO pillar, who they should ask if they have a
  question in that pillar (not you, by default).
- Read the shared-reading commitment and see specific
  dates rather than vague intentions.
- Understand — from the re-planning cadence — that this
  is a living document, not a one-time output.

The plan is the load-bearing artifact of the module. The
lab prompt in this module (see the plan for the module lab
`lab-01-one-page-cto-role-charter-for-your-startup`) will
build on the plan to produce a role charter you can share
with your CEO.
