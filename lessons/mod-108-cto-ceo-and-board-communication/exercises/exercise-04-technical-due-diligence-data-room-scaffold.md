# Exercise 04 — Technical Due-Diligence Data Room Scaffold

**Module:** `mod-108-cto-ceo-and-board-communication`
**Planned time:** ~3 hours
**Chapter this builds on:** [`04-technical-due-diligence-data-room.md`](../04-technical-due-diligence-data-room.md),
supported by
[`mod-107`](../../mod-107-founder-scope-security-and-compliance/README.md)
for the security-posture packet that lands directly in
folder 02, and by
[`mod-102`](../../mod-102-architecture-under-uncertainty/README.md)
for the C4 model and ADR log that anchor folder 01.

## Problem statement

Scaffold the **six-folder DD data room** in your working
repo and populate it with (a) the artifacts you already
own, (b) the artifacts on your roadmap with a target
date, and (c) the *answers-file* covering the recurring
DD questions. Every folder either points at an existing
artifact, points at a scheduled artifact with a date, or
carries an explicit *"why not required at our stage"*
rationale.

The point of the drill is to move the data room from
a *reactive, term-sheet-triggered* build into a
*running artifact* that is a two-week refresh away
from ready on any month of any year. Chapter 04 walks
Pattern A (reactive) and Pattern B (running artifact)
— this exercise installs Pattern B.

This exercise does *not* require you to complete a SOC
2, ship a pen test, or generate a full SBOM as
prerequisites. Where an artifact is not yet produced,
the drill produces the *inventory entry* naming when
and how it will be — the scaffold is what turns the
absence of an artifact into a *scoped* gap rather than
a *discovered* one.

## Requirements

Scaffold the six folders under `docs/dd-data-room/`
(or the equivalent convention in your working repo).
Every folder contains a `README.md` inventory that
enumerates the artifacts, their current status, and
their next-refresh date.

The top-level `docs/dd-data-room/README.md` names the
owner (the CTO), the refresh cadence (quarterly, tied
to the board pre-read cycle from chapter 03), the last-
refreshed date, and the running-artifact discipline
this scaffold installs.

### Folder 01 — Architecture overview

`docs/dd-data-room/01-architecture-overview/`

At minimum, this folder contains or references:

- **`c4-context.md` and `c4-container.md`** — the C4
  context and container diagrams (see chapter 04 and
  [`mod-102`](../../mod-102-architecture-under-uncertainty/README.md)).
  Diagrams may be maintained in a Structurizr / Mermaid
  / draw.io source and rendered into the folder.
- **`architecture-narrative.md`** — the 2-4 page
  narrative of the architecture: language and
  framework, cloud posture, persistence layer,
  deployment topology, multi-tenancy model, and (if
  AI-native) the model stack.
- **`adr-log.md`** — pointer to the ADR log from
  [`mod-102`](../../mod-102-architecture-under-uncertainty/README.md),
  reduced to the DD-material load-bearing decisions
  with the reversibility posture per ADR.
- **`unit-economics.md`** — the current per-tenant /
  per-workload / (for AI-native) per-inference cost,
  with a 10× scaling curve and the largest cost-
  optimisation levers named.
- **`architecture-roadmap.md`** — the 12-18 month
  roadmap of known architectural changes, each
  classified one-way door / two-way door.

The negative-space test — a senior engineer at the
buyer, reading only this folder, can sketch your
system on a whiteboard and hold a 45-minute team
briefing without follow-up questions.

### Folder 02 — Security-posture packet

`docs/dd-data-room/02-security-posture-packet/`

This folder is the *landing site* for the mod-107
compliance package. At minimum:

- **`posture-memo.md`** — pointer to (or copy of)
  the one-page founder-scope posture articulation
  from
  [`mod-107` chapter 01](../../mod-107-founder-scope-security-and-compliance/01-founder-scope-security-posture.md).
- **`soc2/`** — the SOC 2 attestation or readiness
  report, plus the observation-window plan if Type
  II is in flight. If SOC 2 is not yet in scope,
  the folder holds a `not-in-scope.md` naming the
  target quarter or the *"deferred until Series-A"*
  rationale.
- **`pen-test-summary.md`** — the summary letter
  from the most recent third-party pen test, with
  findings by severity and remediation status. The
  full report is a privileged document referenced
  here but shared only under a clean-room NDA.
- **`sbom/`** — a machine-readable SBOM per shipping
  service in
  [CycloneDX](https://cyclonedx.org/) or
  [SPDX](https://spdx.dev/) format, generated by
  [Syft](https://github.com/anchore/syft),
  [cdxgen](https://github.com/CycloneDX/cdxgen), or
  the equivalent.
- **`vendor-inventory.md`** — the sub-processor list
  from
  [`mod-107` chapter 08](../../mod-107-founder-scope-security-and-compliance/08-vendor-dpa-and-baa-acquisition.md)
  with the DPA / BAA reference per vendor.
- **`data-flow-diagram.md`** — the flow diagram for
  each regulated data class (PII / PHI / financial /
  customer-content), with the residency posture per
  class.
- **`asvs-gap-register.md`** — the OWASP ASVS gap
  register from
  [`mod-107` chapter 06](../../mod-107-founder-scope-security-and-compliance/06-owasp-asvs-as-appsec-catalog.md).
- **`slsa-status.md`** — the SLSA build-provenance
  status from
  [`mod-107` chapter 07](../../mod-107-founder-scope-security-and-compliance/07-slsa-and-build-provenance.md).

Every artifact carries a *last-refreshed* date. Stale
artifacts (older than one quarter for
posture-memo / SBOM / vendor-inventory; older than
one year for pen-test summary; auditor-defined for
the SOC 2 report) are flagged in the folder README.

### Folder 03 — IP hygiene

`docs/dd-data-room/03-ip-hygiene/`

The folder most likely to surface hidden findings.
Contents:

- **`ip-assignment-inventory.md`** — a matrix of
  every current and former employee, contractor,
  and founder, cross-referenced against the signed
  IP-assignment / CIIAA agreement. The evidence
  documents themselves are privileged and referenced
  by name and location, not committed to the shared
  repo. Missing signatures are flagged as **P0
  gaps** routed to counsel — see
  [Cooley GO — Confidential Information and Invention Assignment Agreement (CIIAA)](https://www.cooleygo.com/documents/confidential-information-and-invention-assignment-agreement/).
- **`cla-dco-register.md`** — the CLA or DCO status
  for every external contributor to any OSS project
  the company maintains or that ships as a
  load-bearing dependency with a non-standard
  licence. See the
  [Developer Certificate of Origin](https://developercertificate.org/).
- **`oss-licence-audit.md`** — the OSS licence audit
  output. Tools like
  [FOSSA](https://fossa.com/),
  [Snyk licence scanning](https://snyk.io/),
  [ScanCode Toolkit](https://scancode-toolkit.readthedocs.io/),
  or
  [ORT — OSS Review Toolkit](https://oss-review-toolkit.org/)
  produce the machine-readable input; the human
  review of copyleft (GPL / AGPL), source-available
  (SSPL / BSL), and unlicensed dependencies lives
  here.
- **`patents-and-trademarks.md`** — filed and pending
  patent, application, and trademark inventory with
  jurisdiction and status per item.
- **`third-party-licence-survival.md`** — enterprise-
  vendor, reseller, and OEM licences whose terms
  may change on change-of-control.
- **`foundation-model-terms.md`** — for AI-native
  products, the current commercial-use terms of
  every foundation-model provider that touches a
  product surface — anchor references at
  [Anthropic — Commercial Terms of Service](https://www.anthropic.com/legal/commercial-terms),
  [OpenAI — Business Terms](https://openai.com/policies/business-terms/),
  and
  [Google Cloud — Vertex AI Generative AI terms](https://cloud.google.com/vertex-ai/generative-ai/docs/terms/generative-ai-terms).
  Route the legal opinion to counsel; the file
  supplies the *technical fact* of which model
  touched which surface.

### Folder 04 — Engineering org and key-person register

`docs/dd-data-room/04-engineering-org-and-key-persons/`

- **`org-chart.md`** — current org chart with role,
  tenure, and vesting-through-cliff status per person.
  In the shared version, names may be redacted to
  role and identifier; the fully-named version lives
  in the privileged share.
- **`key-person-register.md`** — 3-8 roles at seed /
  Series-A with the specific mitigation per role
  (cross-training, runbooks, hiring-in-flight,
  retention grant, redundancy). Chapter 06 walks the
  three-class board risk register that draws from
  this file.
- **`retention-posture.md`** — the equity-refresh
  cadence for senior engineers, the retention-bonus
  policy, and any current retention plan for named
  key persons.
- **`hiring-plan.md`** — the approved hiring plan
  from the last board meeting, actuals against the
  plan, and the current pipeline by role.
- **`immigration-tracker.md`** — pointer to the
  privileged share for work-authorisation status
  where relevant. Not published to the buyer at
  first pass; tracked internally so the CTO knows
  what the answer is when asked.

### Folder 05 — Incident history and post-mortems

`docs/dd-data-room/05-incident-history-and-postmortems/`

- **`incident-register.md`** — the rolling incident
  log from
  [`mod-106` chapter 07](../../mod-106-scaling-org-and-stack/07-delivery-cadence-and-on-call.md),
  numbered from incorporation, with customer- and
  regulator-notification status per incident.
- **`postmortems/`** — the blameless post-mortem
  for every P0 and P1 incident, in the format the
  SOC 2 Availability criterion measures against.
  Each carries the action-item tracking status.
- **`pattern-analysis.md`** — the CTO's own written
  pattern analysis: recurring classes of incident,
  the investments underway to close each class, and
  the trajectory over the last four quarters.
- **`customer-notifications.md`** — every
  notification issued under an SLA-breach or
  security-notification clause, with notification
  text and customer response captured.
- **`regulator-interactions.md`** — HHS OCR
  interactions under HIPAA, supervisory-authority
  interactions under GDPR, state AG breach
  notifications, or any FTC contact. Route the
  underlying legal opinion via counsel; the file
  captures the fact of the interaction and the
  correspondence log.

### Folder 06 — The answers-file

`docs/dd-data-room/06-answers-file/answers.md`

The single running document that answers the
recurring set of DD questions in the CTO's own voice.
Each answer is 1-3 paragraphs, concrete (specific
vendors, versions, numbers), and dated on a
last-revised line. At minimum, an answer for each
of:

- **The stack** — language, framework, cloud,
  datastore, message bus, background-job system,
  observability stack.
- **The tenancy model** — single-tenant / pooled-
  tenant / multi-tenant hybrid, with data-isolation
  boundaries per tier.
- **The AI/model stack** — foundation-model
  providers, models, zero-retention or BAA-covered
  API modes, self-hosted-vs-API workload split, and
  the fallback posture per primary-provider outage.
- **The data lifecycle** — data classes, storage
  locations, encryption posture, retention posture,
  DSR / DSAR handling, deletion-verification
  process.
- **The deployment model** — release cadence,
  deploys per week, rollback mechanism, change-
  management posture, release-gating process.
- **The observability and on-call posture** —
  detection, on-call rotation, SLA / SLO
  commitments, tooling.
- **The scaling posture** — current scaling
  approach, the next bottleneck, the architectural
  response.
- **The security posture** — short-form (long-form
  lives in folder 02).
- **The compliance posture** — attestations in
  place, in flight, deliberately deferred. Anchored
  to
  [`mod-107` chapter 01](../../mod-107-founder-scope-security-and-compliance/01-founder-scope-security-posture.md).
- **The engineering-team posture** — team size,
  seniority distribution, ownership model, on-call
  distribution, growth plan.

### Meta-artifact — the inventory table

Alongside the per-folder READMEs, the top-level
`docs/dd-data-room/README.md` carries a single
**inventory table** covering every load-bearing
artifact across the six folders:

| Artifact | Folder | Status | Owner | Last-refreshed | Next-refresh | Notes / gap |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| C4 context diagram | 01 | ✅ current | CTO | 2026-08-05 | 2026-11-05 | Rendered from Structurizr source at `docs/architecture/`.  |
| SOC 2 Type I report | 02 | 🟡 in flight | CTO | — | 2026-09-30 | Fieldwork complete; report expected 2026-09-30.  |
| CIIAA — contractor Jane Q. | 03 | 🔴 gap | CEO + counsel | — | 2026-09-15 | Signed CIIAA missing; routed to counsel Aug 20. |
| Key-person register | 04 | ✅ current | CTO | 2026-08-05 | 2026-11-05 |  |
| Incident register (2024→) | 05 | ✅ current | CTO | 2026-08-30 | 2026-11-30 |  |
| Answers-file | 06 | ✅ current | CTO | 2026-08-30 | 2026-11-30 |  |

Status categories: **✅ current** (owned, refreshed
in the last cadence window), **🟡 in flight**
(scheduled, target date named), **🔴 gap** (missing,
owner and target date named). No artifact carries
an unspecified status.

## Starter guidance

- **Scaffold first, populate incrementally.** The
  drill's output is the *scaffold and inventory*,
  not a fully-populated data room. A folder tree
  with `README.md` inventories that name every
  artifact — including the missing ones — is a
  passing output. A partially-populated data room
  with no inventory is not.
- **Reference, do not duplicate.** Where an artifact
  already exists in the working repo (the ADR log,
  the incident register from mod-106, the vendor
  inventory from mod-107), the folder README
  *points at* the source rather than copying. The
  running-artifact discipline depends on there
  being one source of truth per artifact.
- **Privileged materials stay privileged.** Signed
  legal instruments (IP assignments, operating
  agreement, vesting instruments) are not committed
  to the shared repo — they live in the counsel /
  founders' privileged share. The IP-hygiene folder
  *inventories* them by reference; it does not
  contain them.
- **Cite the a16z checklist.** The public
  [a16z technical-DD checklist](https://a16z.com/tech-diligence-checklist/)
  is the load-bearing external reference for the
  six-folder layout. Naming it in the top-level
  README lets a future CTO — or a future you —
  reconstruct the design intent.
- **Sizing the answers-file.** The first pass is
  1-3 paragraphs per question, current on the day
  of writing. Every subsequent DD session
  (exercise 05) adds to the file. A one-year-old
  answers-file with 30 answers written over four
  quarters is a materially stronger artifact than
  a same-day-written 30-answer file.
- **Redact key-person names in the shared version.**
  The DD data room in the shared repo names roles
  and identifiers; the name-mapped version lives
  in the privileged share. Chapter 04 and chapter
  06 both walk this discipline.
- **Do not stage every artifact for a hypothetical
  buyer.** The DD data room is the *company's* running
  artifact first, and a buyer's read second. Stage
  artifacts because they help the founders and the
  board today, not because a hypothetical DD lead
  might ask.

## Acceptance criteria

The drill output is complete when:

- `docs/dd-data-room/` exists with the six named
  sub-folders and a top-level `README.md`.
- Each sub-folder has a `README.md` that inventories
  its load-bearing artifacts and states, per
  artifact, the status (current / in flight /
  gap), owner, and next-refresh date.
- Folder 06 contains a running `answers.md` with
  first-pass answers to each of the ten recurring
  question classes named in the requirements.
- The top-level `README.md` carries the
  cross-folder inventory table with no artifact
  in an unspecified status.
- Every gap in the inventory table has a named
  owner and a target-refresh date. No gap is
  labelled *"TBD"*.
- Privileged legal instruments are referenced but
  not committed to the shared repo.
- The top-level `README.md` names the refresh
  cadence (quarterly), the last-refreshed date,
  and the tie to the board pre-read cycle
  (chapter 03).
- A technical reviewer who does not know your
  company can read the top-level `README.md` and
  the six sub-folder READMEs in 20 minutes and
  answer *"what do they own today, what do they
  owe, and when is each gap closed?"* without
  reading the answers-file.

## What this feeds into

- **Exercise 05** — the hostile-DD response drill
  runs the three-angle attack against the data
  room scaffolded here.
- **Exercise 06** — the board-ready risk narrative
  draws from folder 04 (key-person register) and
  folder 05 (incident pattern analysis) for the
  three-class board risk register.
- **The module lab** consolidates this scaffold
  with exercises 01, 02, 03, 05, 06 into the
  `docs/founder-comms/` sub-tree — the DD data
  room is a peer sub-tree, not a nested one.
- **Capstone
  [`project-103`](../../../projects/project-103-scaling-plan-from-five-to-fifty-engineers)**
  uses the DD data room as the artifact set the
  scaling plan is *audited against* in its
  Series-A / Series-B readiness section.
- **Every future DD engagement** — Series-A
  round, Series-B round, strategic acquisition
  offer — begins from this scaffold rather than
  from an empty folder.

The drill's discipline is *scaffold and inventory
now, populate on the board-pre-read cadence*. The
data room that is *ready* on any month of any year
is the one where every quarterly board update also
refreshes the folder — not the one that is rebuilt
from scratch when a term sheet arrives.
