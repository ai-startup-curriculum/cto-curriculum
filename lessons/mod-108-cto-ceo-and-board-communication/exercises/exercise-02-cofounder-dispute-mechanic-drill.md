# Exercise 02 — Cofounder-Dispute Mechanic Drill

**Module:** `mod-108-cto-ceo-and-board-communication`
**Planned time:** ~2 hours
**Chapter this builds on:** [`02-cofounder-dispute-mechanic.md`](../02-cofounder-dispute-mechanic.md)

## Problem statement

Audit the **four pieces of the cofounder-dispute
mechanic** you actually have installed today —
founding-team equity with vesting, decision-tie-
breaker mechanism (operationally and legally),
cofounder-relationship agreement, and mediation
route — against the checklist in chapter 02.
Produce the **gap list** with each gap routed to
either yourself (for pieces the CTO can author,
like the cofounder-relationship agreement) or to
counsel (for every legal instrument).

The point of the drill is not to *become* the
company's lawyer. The point is to audit the
mechanic honestly, name every gap explicitly,
and give counsel a defensible package of
questions rather than a vague *"we should check
our documents"*. See chapter 02's strict boundary
to counsel — the CTO owns the specification of
what each instrument must do and the reading of
the returned draft against that specification;
counsel owns the drafting.

## Requirements

Author a document at
`docs/founder-comms/cofounder-dispute-mechanic-audit.md`
(or the equivalent convention in your working
repo). The document is a *founder-facing* audit
and gap list; it is not a legal opinion.

### Section 1 — Context (150-250 words)

- The founder pair, roles, and tenure.
- The company's legal structure (Delaware C-corp,
  LLC, jurisdiction) and stage (pre-seed / seed
  / Series-A). If you are working from a
  reference startup, state that explicitly.
- The current existence of legal counsel — do
  you have startup-experienced counsel of record
  today? If not, name that as the first gap.
- Any prior disagreements between founders (in
  the abstract — not on-the-record incidents)
  and how they were resolved; whether the
  founders would characterise their working
  relationship today as *high-trust*, *typical*,
  or *strained*. This is context for the reader
  of the audit, not evidence.

### Section 2 — Piece 1: founding-team equity with vesting

State, item by item, what is currently installed
and where the evidence lives:

- **Equity split.** What is the current percentage
  split between the founders? Is it documented in
  the operating agreement or the shareholders'
  agreement? Reference the file / document.
- **Vesting schedule.** What is the current
  schedule (four-year / one-year cliff /
  monthly? Deviation from that norm)? Reference
  the vesting instrument (restricted-stock
  purchase agreement, option agreement).
- **Acceleration provisions.** Is single- or
  double-trigger acceleration in place? What
  triggers *cause* and *good reason*? Reference.
- **Reverse-vesting on founder shares.** Is the
  reverse-vesting / repurchase-right in place?
  What is the price mechanism? Reference.
- **83(b) election filed.** (US-jurisdiction
  founders only.) Has each founder filed the
  election within 30 days of grant? Where is
  the evidence?
- **IP assignment.** Every founder assigns pre-
  formation and formation-period IP to the
  company. Where is the evidence?

For each item, state one of: **installed and
documented** (with the reference), **installed
but not documented** (routed to counsel), or
**not installed** (routed to counsel).

### Section 3 — Piece 2: decision-tie-breaker mechanism

State, for each of the operational and legal
tie-breaker mechanisms:

- **Operational tie-breaker in the weekly 1:1**
  — pointer to `docs/founder-comms/decision-
  rights-map.md` (from exercise 01) with an
  explicit note that every consensus-column row
  has a named tie-breaker.
- **Board composition.** Does the current board
  have a *neutral third seat* (independent
  director or agreed board observer) that
  breaks a two-founder deadlock? If not, name
  the gap.
- **Voting agreements** in place (if any
  investors are on the cap table). Reference
  the investor rights agreement / voting
  agreement. Route to counsel for the read on
  which decisions are routed to investor
  consent under protective provisions.
- **Statutory-deadlock provisions** in the
  operating agreement. Route to counsel for the
  read.

### Section 4 — Piece 3: cofounder-relationship agreement

Does the cofounder-relationship agreement (the
*non-legal* companion) exist today? For each of
the five sections named in chapter 02 (roles,
values, working style, decision rights, exit
conditions), state:

- **Installed and current** — the section
  exists, was drafted with your cofounder,
  and has been reviewed within the last 12
  months.
- **Installed but stale** — the section exists
  but has not been reviewed since a material
  event (a new hire, a fundraise, a role
  change, a dispute). State the last-review
  date.
- **Not installed** — the section does not
  exist and belongs on the drill action list.

If the cofounder-relationship agreement does
not exist at all, that is the top item on the
drill action list. It is a document the CTO
*can* author with the CEO in the honeymoon
period — this is not the counsel-drafted piece.
Draft a first cut (a section outline, or a
short draft under 1,500 words) as part of the
drill.

### Section 5 — Piece 4: mediation route

Name, for each of the three rungs of the
escalation ladder in chapter 02:

- **Informal advisor / founder-coach.** A
  specific named person, or the action item to
  identify one. If a person is named, have
  they agreed to the role? Have both founders
  been introduced?
- **Board / lead director.** A specific named
  person on the current board or advisory
  board. If the board does not yet exist,
  point at the escalation-column entry from
  exercise 01 and name what will fill this
  rung.
- **Formal mediator / counsel-led separation.**
  This is the *last resort* rung. It does not
  need to have a named individual today, but
  it must have a named *route* — the specific
  counsel who would be engaged, or the process
  by which counsel would be selected.

Every rung must either be filled with a name or
be on the action list as *"identify by [date]"*.
A rung with no filled name and no action item
is a failed requirement.

### Section 6 — Gap list, routed

A prioritised list of the gaps identified in
Sections 2-5. For each gap:

- The gap in one sentence.
- The section of the audit it came from.
- The **routing**: either the CTO (typically
  for cofounder-relationship-agreement pieces
  and mediation-route identifications) or
  **counsel** (for every legal instrument or
  legal-opinion question).
- The **priority**: *P0 — install before next
  material event* (fundraise, executive hire,
  board addition); *P1 — install this quarter*;
  *P2 — install this year*.
- The **acceptance criterion** — how you will
  know the gap is closed.

Every gap routed to counsel becomes a *written
question* rather than a chat request. See chapter
02 — routing to counsel with a specification
produces a defensible package that returns as
a drafted instrument; routing to counsel with a
vague *"can you look at this"* produces
open-ended billing and thin output.

### Section 7 — Counsel engagement plan

If startup-experienced counsel of record does
not exist today, this section names the plan to
retain them. Reference the templated starting
points chapter 02 named — Y Combinator startup
documents at
[ycombinator.com/documents](https://www.ycombinator.com/documents),
Cooley GO at
[cooleygo.com/documents](https://www.cooleygo.com/documents/),
the CIIAA template at
[cooleygo.com — CIIAA](https://www.cooleygo.com/documents/confidential-information-and-invention-assignment-agreement/) —
as anchoring instruments for the counsel
conversation. State the counsel-selection process
and the expected timeline.

If counsel of record already exists, this section
becomes the *engagement request* — the
prioritised gap list you would send counsel to
open the audit engagement, with a sizing
estimate for the work.

## Starter guidance

- **The audit is honest, not aspirational.**
  Every *"installed"* claim must have an
  evidence reference. If you cannot point at
  the document, the item is *not installed
  and not documented*.
- **Route to counsel *with specification*.**
  The gap list to counsel names *what the
  instrument must do*, not *what clauses to
  write*. Chapter 02's discipline: CTO owns
  the specification, counsel owns the drafting.
  A gap-list entry to counsel that reads
  *"draft us an operating agreement"* is
  under-specified; one that reads *"the
  operating agreement should have a named
  tie-breaker mechanism for governance
  deadlocks at the founder level, currently
  the cap table is [X], the board is [Y]"* is
  a defensible package.
- **The cofounder-relationship agreement is
  the piece you can author.** Chapter 02's
  five-section framing (roles, values,
  working style, decision rights, exit
  conditions) is the outline; the Founder
  Institute template at
  [fi.co/insight/founder-agreements](https://fi.co/insight/founder-agreements)
  is a starting point. Draft it *with* your
  cofounder in the honeymoon period — a
  document one founder writes alone and hands
  to the other is not a relationship
  agreement.
- **Do not draft the legal instruments
  yourself.** Direct CTO edits to the
  operating agreement, the vesting instrument,
  or the founder-shareholders' agreement are
  an anti-pattern. Every edit goes through
  counsel.
- **Filed 83(b) elections are time-limited.**
  If the audit surfaces a missed 83(b)
  election window, that is a permanent hole in
  the founder's personal tax posture, not an
  auditable-later item. Route to counsel
  immediately — even if the remediation is
  *"no remediation possible, note the
  outcome"*.
- **Do not commit the actual legal
  instruments to the shared repo without
  privileged-material handling**. The
  operating agreement, the shareholders'
  agreement, and the vesting instruments
  contain privileged material. The audit
  *references* them; the documents themselves
  live in the privileged share the counsel
  and the founders control.

## Acceptance criteria

The drill output is complete when:

- The audit exists at
  `docs/founder-comms/cofounder-dispute-
  mechanic-audit.md`.
- Section 2 states, for every item in the
  vesting checklist, whether the item is
  installed-and-documented, installed-but-
  not-documented, or not-installed, with an
  evidence reference for every documented
  item.
- Section 3 states the operational and legal
  tie-breaker mechanisms with the board-
  composition and voting-agreement gaps
  routed to counsel.
- Section 4 states the state of every
  section of the cofounder-relationship
  agreement, with a first-cut draft of any
  missing section that the CTO can author
  alone.
- Section 5 fills every rung of the
  mediation-route escalation ladder with a
  named person, or a specific action item to
  identify one.
- Section 6 is a **prioritised, routed** gap
  list with an acceptance criterion per gap.
- Section 7 names a specific counsel-
  engagement plan (retention or engagement
  request).
- No legal instrument is drafted in the CTO's
  hand; every legal-instrument gap is routed
  to counsel with a specification.

## What this feeds into

- **The cofounder-relationship agreement**
  drafted in Section 4 is a durable artifact
  the founders return to in every 1:1 retro
  and in every board-composition change.
- **The counsel engagement** initiated in
  Section 7 produces the drafted or updated
  legal instruments (operating agreement
  amendments, vesting-instrument refresh,
  IP-assignment amendments) that back the
  operational decision-rights map from
  exercise 01.
- **The module lab** consolidates this audit
  with the outputs of exercises 01, 03-06
  into the `docs/founder-comms/` sub-tree.
- **Capstone
  [`project-103`](../../../projects/project-103-scaling-plan-from-five-to-fifty-engineers)**
  uses the audit as an input to the governance
  section of the scaling plan.

The drill's discipline is *audit, do not
draft*. The CTO who returns from the drill with
a scoped, routed, prioritised gap list has
compressed the future dispute-mechanic
engagement with counsel by an order of
magnitude. The CTO who returns with a drafted
operating-agreement amendment has stepped
outside the seat.
